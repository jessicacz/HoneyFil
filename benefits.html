Marketing vs evidence

How to interpret product claims

A useful distinction is between what a product is marketed around and what has actually been established through product-specific evidence. Marketing language can explain positioning, but it does not by itself establish effectiveness, safety or a medical outcome.

When a claim matters to your decision, look for current product documentation and, where relevant, independent evidence that directly concerns the finished product rather than only an individual ingredient.

Simple rule

If a statement comes from product marketing, describe it as a product claim or positioning statement. Do not turn it into a fact simply by repeating it.

Practical use

Questions to Ask Before Relying on a Benefit Claim

Is the claim product-specific?

Check whether the evidence concerns the finished HoneyFil product or only a general ingredient.

Is the product version current?

Compare the claim with the current label and product information because formulas and presentation can change.

Is the language absolute?

Words such as “guaranteed” or “works for everyone” require particular caution and should not be assumed from promotional copy.

Is the source independent?

Consider whether the information comes from product marketing, a personal review or an independent evidence source.
